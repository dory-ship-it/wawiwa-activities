Class practice: audit your own build as a system

Module 4 · Vibe Coding  ·  Lesson 5  ·  Stage A 20 min, Stage B 20 min

In pairs, on something one of you built in Lesson 2, 3 or 4. 45 minutes. Dummy data only. A question you cannot answer is a finding, not a failure.
Stage A — the structure, 20 minutes
01

Your entities, and one of them in full

List the entities in your system. Then take one and write every field on it, with its type.

Entity is the kind of thing the system manages. A record is one of them. A field is one detail on it.

Keep it about the work. No names, email addresses, phone numbers or anything else personal, yours or anyone else’s.
02

One relationship, and whether the tool knows it

Name one relationship between two entities, then go and check whether the system actually holds it.

A relationship you can see on a screen is not the same as one the data knows about.

Keep it about the work. No names, email addresses, phone numbers or anything else personal, yours or anyone else’s.
03

The wrong field, and the unwritten rule

Find one field whose type is wrong, or one the tool added unasked. Then name a rule your system is meant to enforce that nobody ever wrote down.

The unwritten rule is the one that bites later: it lives in somebody’s head, not in the system.

Keep it about the work. No names, email addresses, phone numbers or anything else personal, yours or anyone else’s.
Stage B — the evidence, 20 minutes
04

The evidence: the data area and the log

Confirm a record you added is really in the data area. Then open the log and find the most recent failed action.

A guess is what you think happened. Evidence is the line in the log that says what did.

Keep it about the work. No names, email addresses, phone numbers or anything else personal, yours or anyone else’s.
05

The risk, the measure, and one fix request

Which of the six data-integrity failures could happen here? Which single event would tell you the system is being used? Then write one evidence-based fix request — and do not send it yet.

Keep it about the work. No names, email addresses, phone numbers or anything else personal, yours or anyone else’s.
