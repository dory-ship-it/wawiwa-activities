Stage B — pair review (15 min)
05

Pair review

15 minutes, ideally with someone from a different industry. Each of you presents your three scenarios to the other.

  • Was an obvious scenario missed? Is there another self-evident scenario that follows directly from your partner's architecture?
  • Does the guardrail really stop the problem? Is the control defined specifically for this scenario, or too generic (“we will instruct the system to be careful”)?
  • Is it the right stakeholder? Is the problem escalated to someone actually authorized to handle it, or is there a more suitable stakeholder?
  • Is direct and indirect confused? Confirm that at least one scenario is direct and at least one is indirect, and that the difference is defined correctly.
Don't include personal or identifying information in your answers.
Stage C — plenary discussion (30 min)
06

Plenary discussion

  • What repeats across the cases?
  • Which guardrail was most common? Which type of control appeared most often across the class, and why do you think that is?
  • A scenario that is hard to classify? Did anyone find a scenario that is hard to map to one of the three core categories, and which of the other seven does it belong to?
Don't include personal or identifying information in your answers.

Expected output: a complete table of at least three scenarios with all six columns filled, one per participant. It is the direct basis for the home practice.

Doing this exercise does not guarantee a secure system: it is an initial risk-identification stage, not a warranty. The guardrails you define here are a starting point and will be reviewed again during the build stages.
