23 Nov 2026. Slides 1 to 11 were worked through with Noy in two rounds; slides 12 to 23 are a first proposal. v1 (`PLAN.md`) stays for reference. "Kept from slide N" marks a screen that keeps a well-built slide's own structure.

**The thread through the lesson**: at the start the learner picks one pain from their own top ten (L2). Each chapter ends by applying its tool to that pain. At the end, card 1 is ready and the workbook holds the other nine.

**The growing card** (Noy, 23 Nov 2026): the learner's own card (slide 17's fields) goes with them through the lesson. It is shown empty at the end of Part B ("this is the card you will fill today, field by field"). Every chapter opens with it, and the field (or fields) this chapter fills lights up, so it is clear what we are about to fill. The fields already filled show the learner's own words. At each "Your pain" step the field fills in front of them. At the end of Part I the card is complete. It appears at chapter openings and fill moments only, not as a fixed widget on every screen (no repeated chrome).

## Part F. Find the root cause (slide 12)
Slide 12 teaches how to find the real cause behind a pain before choosing a solution (the card's Root Cause field). It stacks three tools: 5-Whys (ask "why" again and again until you reach the cause), Fishbone (the pain as the fish's head, six bones for six kinds of cause), Pareto (most of the pain comes from a few causes, so focus there). Noy's direction: say there are three tools, then one screen per tool, each as an attractive infographic that keeps the learner's attention. Motion here shows the method itself, so it is allowed (once, short, none with reduced motion).
- **F0 Opener: "Three tools to find the root cause"** (learn, dark). The growing card with Root Cause lit. Three tiles: 5-Whys, Fishbone, Pareto, each with one line (what it does) and a small icon of its own shape (a descending chain, a fish skeleton, a short bar chart).
- **F1 5-Whys: "Dig down"** (learn, infographic). **Kept from slide 12**: the slide's own chain, drawn as a shaft going down from the surface. At the top, the symptom (support replies take more than 48 hours). - **F2 5-Whys: your turn** (do, scored). The trainer's own example from the slide 12 notes, which is also the deck's model card OP-014: invoices are processed slowly. The root: a modern OCR re-evaluation, not more headcount.
- **F3 Fishbone: "The six bones"** (learn, infographic). **Kept from slide 12's diagram**, drawn in motion: the head appears first with the pain (support reply takes more than 48 hours), the spine draws back from it, then the six bones grow one after another, each lighting up with its name and one line on what to look for (People: skills, ownership, capacity; Process: steps, handoffs; Tools: systems, integrations; Data: quality, availability; Policy: rules, SLAs, approvals; External: customers, vendors, regulators, from the booklet). ## Part G. Who hurts? (slide 13)
Slide 13 says: every pain has an owner, and not only the process owner: the person who actually feels the pain when it hits. Two reasons to name them: (1) the cleanest signal of value, because the person who hurts will put a number on it for you, often without being asked; (2) the ally for change, because solutions stick when the person who hurts is part of the fix, and die when the fix is imposed on someone who never asked for it. So: a Pain Owner field on every card, "who feels this most?" as a routine interview question, and in the executive deck the Pain Owner on the same line as the person who carries the action. Slide 13 is prose, so it is rebuilt. Learners already wrote Pain Owners on the L1 and L2 worksheets, so the chapter builds on that.
**Slimmed (Noy asked if it is needed, 23 Nov 2026)**: it is needed: Pain Owner is a field on every card, the module's cross-cutting V2 field, knowledge check Q19, and L6 names the Pain Owner in the executive deck. But learners already wrote Pain Owners in L1 and L2, so it gets **one screen**, placed right after the root cause (first why it hurts, then who it hurts), plus one line in the "Your pain" step. G0 and G1 below are merged into that one screen: G0's two figures and two reasons on top, then only G1's round 2 (which Pain Owner passes review) as its scored question. Round 1 is dropped.
