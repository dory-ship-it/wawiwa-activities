# EAI M2 L3 self-study: the plan (v1, for Noy's approval)

Story-led, the Implementor profile. The case is **NorthRiver Holdings**, the case the learners met in
L1 and L2 in class. The cast: Sarah Chen (CFO), Henrik (finance analyst), Marcus Holloway (property
operations), Diana Vasquez (leasing), Robert Pak (general counsel), Lin Zhao (IT). The lesson follows
the order a card is thought through: the pain, then the cause, then who hurts, then the category,
then whether it can ship, then the card. The learner leaves with a workbook of 10 cards.

About 23 screens, roughly 60 to 70 minutes, plus the workbook. 12 scored questions make up the score
(pass mark 80, one try, no retake). There is no final check: the module has its own knowledge check.
Facts and sources: `FACTS.md`.

## Opening
**Title.** The deck's cover, its slide 1 photo, the quote "AI is the tool. Value is the goal."
**Where we are.** The six Module 2 lessons as stations: L1 interviews and L2 As-Is (both in class) behind, today L3 on your own, then L4 prioritizes the cards you make today. The learner opens the stations. One line says what they will produce: 10 opportunity cards in a workbook. Clean light ground, no photo.

## Chapter 1. Every pain becomes a card (slides 2 to 4)
**Film 1, "Why this and not that?"** (about 60 s, one stop, scored). A NorthRiver steering meeting: Sarah Chen reads an earlier consultant's report and asks why one recommendation and not another. Nobody can say which pain it came from. The stop asks what would have answered her. The film ends on the chain: recommendation, pain, quote, meeting date.
**Key idea** (dark glass, blurred photo). Slides 2 and 4 together: "Every pain becomes a card. No exceptions." The chain from the film as four linked steps; the learner opens each link to see what it holds.
**Pain or opportunity?** (two draft cards one after the other, scored). The trainer's most common stumble: is this a real opportunity, or the pain said again? Robert's "no contract metadata" turned into "add contract metadata" is the pain said again. Light ground, blurred photo of a desk with contract binders.

## Chapter 2. Process first, then the tool (slides 5 to 11)
**Key idea** (dark, photo). Slide 5's gate: a broken process with a tool on top makes the flaw run faster ("paving the cow path"). Process redesign comes first and sits in front of the four categories. One short animation draws the gate in front of the four categories, once.
**The four categories** (open the tiles, then one scored question). Slide 6's four cards plus Process Redesign in front. Each tile opens with its deck line and one NorthRiver example. After all of them, the heading turns into a question: which category fits Diana's "listing copy for 84 properties, 2-person team"? Dot ground.
**Sort the pains** (Rise-style sorting, scored). Replaces the class roundtable: six NorthRiver pains go into five piles. Two are boundary cases, and their feedback names the dominant category (slide 6 note: pick the dominant one). Example: "the Northeast does not use the CRM at all" goes to Process Redesign, not to a tool. Light ground, no photo.
**The use-case library** (three cases one after the other, scored). Slides 7, 9 and 11 as the library, sorted by function. For three NorthRiver pains the learner picks the closest library case, as the NotebookLM lookup does (slide 20). Slides 8 and 10, the spotlights, appear as one model of how deep a card goes: stack, effort, payback in month 8, watch-out. Blurred photo.

## Chapter 3. Find the root cause (slides 12, 13)
**Film 2, "The Boston heating failure"** (about 90 s, two stops, scored). Marcus tells the story: one vendor double-booked by two regions, a cold building, 40 units of rent refunded. The 5-Whys run on it. Stop 1: which "why" comes next. Stop 2: what is the root cause (no shared view of a shared vendor). The answer is master data and scheduling first, AI second (knowledge check Q13).
**Fishbone** (drag, scored). The Boston pain as a Fishbone: six candidate causes are dragged onto People, Process, Tools, Data, Policy, External (slide 12's diagram). This is a new board, built on the flags template. Clean light ground.
**The heaviest branch** (one choice, scored). Which branch weighs most, and what does it mean for the category (the booklet's rule: Data heavy means BI or extraction, Process heavy means redesign first...). "Pareto" is an underlined term with a tooltip. Blurred photo (a boiler room, hands).
**Who hurts? Your Pain Owner** (input, not scored; the learner's own thread). Slide 13 in one line at the top (the person who feels it is the value signal and the change ally), then the learner writes the Pain Owner and the Sponsor for the process they mapped in L2's home practice. It feeds the coach and the workbook. Blurred photo, look A.

## Chapter 4. Can it ship? (slides 14 to 16)
**Sort data into tiers** (Rise-style sorting, scored). Six NorthRiver items into Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted. Listing copy goes to Public, the org chart to Internal, performance reviews to Confidential (knowledge check Q4), tenant payment details to Restricted. Dot ground, no photo.
**What Restricted means** (one choice, scored). The card reads Restricted: what does that do to the tool and the procurement path? On-prem or compliant cloud, plus legal review (Q6). The wrong options include "anonymize and use a consumer account". Blurred photo.
**Governance, briefly** (dark key idea, not scored). Slide 15's four corners on one screen, each one line. A pointer says Module 3 and Module 6 teach them. The learner opens the corners.
**Integration score** (score four dimensions, not scored). For Diana's lead-routing opportunity, the learner scores API, bulk data, SSO and deployment 0 to 2 from facts the case gives (Lin: no SSO across 8+ systems). The sum lands on the card, with slide 16's line: a 9 out of 10 opportunity can still take six months instead of six weeks. Clean light ground. May need a small new board (or the DM builder).

## Chapter 5. Build the card (slides 17 to 21)
**Robert's card, field by field** (choose one line per field, scored). Slide 17's format, filled for Robert's contracts: pain, Pain Owner, root cause, opportunity, category, sensitivity, and the explicit "do not" line (no AI clause review, metadata only; Q12). One Check after all fields, one try, then the reveal. Document view.
**Review the AI draft** (flags, scored). Slide 18: an AI-drafted card for a NorthRiver pain, with its prompt shown as context. The learner drags flags onto the fields that need correcting: the opportunity repeats the pain, the Owner is "TBD", payment data is tagged Internal. The line: "AI is fast at draft, slower at calibration." Focus wash.
**Three more AI checks** (open the tiles, not scored). Slides 19 to 21: classify and compare with the AI, the NotebookLM lookup, the sensitivity auto-flag, each in three steps, plus the module's rule (no client data in consumer AI accounts). They are practised in the home practice. Blurred photo.

## Chapter 6. Your 10 cards (slides 22, 23)
**Open your workbook** (handoff). The 10 NorthRiver pains and the targets from the booklet: at least 4 departments, 3 of the 4 categories, 2 Restricted cards, 1 process redesign. Then the Fishbone for one pain. Open workbook, the return line, a small preview of the card. Document view.
**Check your own cards** (self-review, not scored). Replaces the class peer review: the booklet's checklist as Yes / Not yet, with a fix line for each "Not yet" and Reopen workbook. Blurred photo.
**Close** (three takeaways, then the bridge). Slide 23: in L4 you prioritize everything you made today, so bring every card and do not delete the weak ones. Open home practice (5 more cards, the AI checks). Photo of a meeting room from behind.
**End.** Finish.
