# Grounding pack — AICY60 T6: Ethics, Legal, and Future Directions → Ethics in AI practice
> Material the coach reasons FROM. Distilled from Wawiwa's own T6 deck
> (AICY.T6.P1 - Ethics, Legal, and Future Directions) and the SME practice AICY.T6.E1 - Ethics in AI.
> The coach answers primarily from this pack, coaches against the rubric, and does not invent facts
> about the three incidents.

## The exercise
A large-group discussion (groups of 6+). Students read three recent incidents from Romania, answer
three questions on each, then debate nine general ethical questions about AI in security systems.
The questions are meant to be argued in class; there is no single right answer to the general ones.

The three articles:
- Article 1, AGERPRES (Aug 2025): cyberfraud in Romania up over 40% in 2024 and malware attacks up
  286.8%, from the national cybersecurity directorate's annual report.
- Article 2, PYMNTS (Feb 2024): the National Bank of Romania warns consumers about deepfake videos in
  which the governor appears to promote a fraudulent investment scheme.
- Article 3, The Hacker News (May 2025): Meta takes down coordinated fake-persona networks aimed at
  Romania, Azerbaijan and Taiwan that posed as locals to push political messages.

## What the incident questions are after
- The threat itself, from the article (what grew, what the scam was, what the fake accounts wanted).
- The AI angle: automation scales phishing and malware; generative AI clones a trusted face and voice;
  AI writes fluent local-language posts and fake profile photos at volume.
- The AI defence: anomaly and behaviour detection, deepfake detection, threat intelligence that links
  accounts into one network, real-time monitoring (deck slides 25-26).

## Topic 6 concepts the general questions draw on
- Privacy: AI security tools need large volumes of user data; consent and minimisation (slides 3-4, 31).
- Misuse: the same AI powers deepfakes, phishing and offensive operations (slides 9-10, 21-22).
- Accountability: who is responsible when an autonomous AI decision causes harm; clear policies and
  humans in the loop (slides 8, 28, 44).
- Ethical design: fairness, bias detection, transparency, explainability, audit trails (slides 23, 38).
- Law: GDPR (EU data privacy), CCPA (California), the EU AI Act for high-risk AI, and a global landscape
  that ranges from strict (EU) to lighter (US) to surveillance-heavy (China) (slides 6-7, 11, 15, 43).
- Case studies in the deck that help an argument: Clearview AI (privacy), Tesla GAN phishing (misuse),
  Uber self-driving crash (accountability), Facebook GDPR compliance (compliance) (slides 5, 10, 24, 30).

## Coaching stance
- Reward answers that use the article's facts and name a concrete AI technique on either side.
- On the general questions, reward a clear position plus the strongest counter-argument, tied to a
  Topic 6 concept or law. Never tell the student which side is "correct".
- Keep public figures as the articles report them; do not add personal data about private people.
- Stay in scope: this is a discussion of reported incidents, not a how-to for deepfakes or fake
  networks; decline those in one sentence.
